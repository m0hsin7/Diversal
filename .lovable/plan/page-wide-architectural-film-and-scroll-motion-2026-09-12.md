# Page-wide architectural film and scroll motion

## Direction
- Replace the current hero footage with a polished 10-second cinematic architectural loop: a continuous, slow aerial passage through a cool-toned glass-and-stone city district, with stable camera movement and no people, text, or synthetic effects.
- Carry the film from the opening through the footer as one continuous visual layer rather than placing separate videos inside every section.
- Keep information readable by alternating transparent, porcelain, and deep-ink section treatments over the film; use depth scrims only where necessary.

## Scroll experience
- Tie the background film’s scale, position, contrast, and opacity to page scroll so it subtly evolves through every section.
- Add section-specific scroll transforms to headings, property imagery, buying-option details, process steps, proof figures, FAQ rows, and footer content.
- Preserve the existing pinned process sequence while making its transitions more cinematic and connected to the underlying film.
- Keep motion limited to transforms and opacity, using the existing shared easing.

## Mobile and accessibility
- Use the poster image instead of continuous video on smaller screens and for reduced-motion users.
- Simplify page-wide effects to gentle reveal transitions on mobile.
- Keep all content and controls fully readable and usable over the moving background.

## Verification
- Check the full page at desktop and mobile sizes, including section transitions, video playback, footer arrival, reduced-motion behavior, text contrast, and browser errors.
